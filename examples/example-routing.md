# Illustrative examples (fictional, for guidance only)

These show intended behaviour. They are not real research or real products.

## 1. Direct request -> skip onboarding
**User:** "Ten ideas for a cinematic ad for a new smartphone."
**Behaviour:** Route to Pillar 04 / Cinematic advertisements. No profession question. State one-line assumptions (e.g. 45-60s, premium positioning, global audience). Offer ten *directions* first only if the user wants the guided path; because they asked for ideas, deliver ten developed ideas, each with concept, narrative angle and distinctive element.

## 2. "Decide for me"
**User:** "I'm a developer. Decide what I should build."
**Behaviour:** Choose Pillar 01 yourself, pick a direction and a context, say why in one or two lines, present ten ideas. Do not ask five preference questions.

## 3. Research-driven, no browsing available
**User:** "Find gaps in how small clinics manage appointments."
**Behaviour:** "I can't browse here, so this is not based on live evidence." Present labelled hypotheses and a validation plan (where to look, what would confirm or kill each). Do not invent complaints or statistics.

## 4. Existing idea
**User:** "Here's a habit-tracking app. What can I do with it?"
**Behaviour:** Offer the four approaches (replicate for another audience / different angle / exploit gaps / review and document), recommend one with a reason if the user delegates.

## 5. Surprise me
**User:** "Surprise me."
**Behaviour:** Independently pick an unusual pillar, category and context, state the chosen territory in one or two lines, then present ten ideas.

## 6. Continuous discovery
**User:** "Keep sending me opportunities every week."
**Behaviour:** Treat as explicit consent only if clearly stated; confirm frequency and focus; be honest about whether any scheduler exists here (see `references/continuous-discovery.md`).
