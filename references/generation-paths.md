# The Four Generation Paths

Independent entry points that can also work together. Pick the path from the user's intent; switch whenever they ask.

## Path 1: Original Ideas
Purpose: independently conceive new, distinctive ideas.
Process: understand intent -> choose pillar, category, subcategory -> explore interesting directions -> present ten directions -> user selects -> ten ideas in that direction -> user selects -> developed brief -> post-brief explanation.
When the user delegates industry, audience or direction, choose them yourself and say what you chose in one line.

## Path 2: Evidence-Based / Research-Driven Ideas
Purpose: discover opportunities from what is actually happening in the world. This is not generating ideas and adding research vocabulary. Follow `research-protocol.md` fully.
Before the ten ideas or directions, deliver an **opportunity context**: what you investigated, what you found, what is currently done, what appears missing, the opportunity you identified, why it deserves exploration. Then present ten directions or ideas depending on the stage requested.
If you cannot browse, say so and fall back to labelled hypotheses (never present them as findings).

## Path 3: Existing-Idea Exploration
Input may be an app, website, business, brand, physical product, creative concept, film, book, ad, technology or anything else. Offer four approaches (let the user pick; if they delegate, choose the most promising and say why):

**A. Replicate for another audience.** Reimagine for a different demographic, geography, industry, segment, underserved population or professional group. Adapt the underlying value proposition; never blindly copy.

**B. Approach from a different angle.** Alternative positioning, mechanism, experience, business model, distribution, creative execution, technology or delivery. Preserve the useful underlying opportunity.

**C. Exploit gaps and limitations.** Investigate missing capabilities, weak UX, unserved audiences, poor accessibility, inefficient workflows, pricing and technical limits, distribution weaknesses, unmet adjacent needs. Turn gaps into new concepts or better alternatives. Gaps must be observed or evidenced; label inferred ones as hypotheses.

**D. Review and document.** Structured, analytical, fair, evidence-aware review:
1. What the idea is. 2. What it attempts. 3. Who it serves. 4. What works. 5. Why it works. 6. What does not work. 7. Limitations. 8. What could be improved. 9. Lessons. 10. New opportunities.

Do not assume replication is always right. Respect intellectual property; distinguish inspiration from unauthorised copying.

## Path 4: Surprise Me
Purpose: independently discover a direction worth exploring.
Do: select a pillar independently; choose an interesting category; identify an unusual subcategory; choose an unexpected industry or creative context; explore an unexpected direction; generate ten ideas; present them.
Avoid the popular industries and familiar ideas by default. Favour unusual combinations, emerging disciplines, neglected problems and unexpected creative territories. Before the ideas, state in one or two lines what territory you chose and why it is worth the user's attention. The goal is to surprise with something worth exploring, not novelty for its own sake.

## Combining paths
Example: Path 2 research feeds Path 1 ideas; Path 3 review feeds Path 4 surprise. Preserve context across switches.
