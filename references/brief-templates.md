# Brief Templates (Stage 6)

## General structure
Every developed brief begins with a **title card**, then an **introductory narrative**, then the discipline sections.

### Title card (include only relevant fields)
PROJECT TITLE / PROJECT TYPE / PILLAR / CATEGORY / SUBCATEGORY / INDUSTRY / TARGET AUDIENCE / PRIMARY OBJECTIVE / CREATIVE DIRECTION / FORMAT

### Opening
Choose by the nature of the project; do not use the same opening every time. Examples: "This project is...", "Imagine...", "What if...", "Just imagine...", "Consider a world where...", "The concept explores...".
Tone: a cinematic concept feels cinematic; a brand concept reads as a creative direction; a product brief as a considered product opportunity; a business brief as strategically and commercially intelligent; a book concept as intellectually compelling or narratively engaging.

### Rules
- Use only sections that are relevant. Never pad with irrelevant sections to satisfy a template.
- Preserve the selected idea's core premise.
- Be detailed enough that the user understands what they will create.
- A concept brief is not a PRD, screenplay, full brand book or business plan unless the user asks for that.
- Mark assumptions. Mark research-backed claims with their evidence label and source.

## Discipline templates

### Digital products and technology
Product name; premise; problem; opportunity; target users; existing alternatives; proposed solution; core value proposition; product experience; key functionality; differentiation; user journey; platform; technical considerations; potential business model; feasibility considerations; future possibilities.

### Branding
Brand name; premise; purpose; market context; target audience; positioning; personality; differentiation; brand story; visual identity direction; verbal identity direction; applications; creative opportunities.

### Film and short film
Title; format; genre; logline; concept introduction; synopsis; story premise; themes; characters; setting; conflict; narrative structure; emotional direction; visual treatment; sound direction; cinematic opportunities; intended audience. (Screenplay only if requested.)

### Advertising and cinematic video ads
Campaign title; advertiser or product; objective; target audience; central concept; core message; creative approach; narrative; storyboard direction; visual treatment; cinematography; sound and music direction; duration; production considerations; intended audience response.

### Music and audio
Concept title; genre or style; premise; mood; emotional direction; reference characteristics (general, never copying distinctive expression of existing works); instrumentation; tempo or energy; structure; vocal direction; sound design; intended listening experience.

### Music video
Video title; music context; concept premise; visual narrative; artistic direction; scene progression; environment; performer direction; cinematography; editing; visual effects; colour grading; sound synchronisation; production requirements.

### Photography
Project title; concept; subject; visual premise; artistic direction; composition; lighting; colour treatment; camera perspective; environment; styling; mood; production considerations; intended application.

### Image generation and visual art
Artwork title; concept; subject; visual premise; style; composition; colour language; environment; lighting; materials; texture; perspective; visual details; intended format; generation or production considerations. (Make it detailed enough to serve as a production prompt when handed to another tool.)

### Writing and publishing
Working title; format; central idea; core thesis or narrative; intended reader; reader problem or interest; unique perspective; content structure; chapters or sections; tone and voice; research requirements; reader outcome; publishing format; applications.
- Commercial writing: emphasise persuasion, audience, offer, message, positioning, intended action.
- Literary writing: narrative, characters, conflict, themes, structure, emotional experience.
- Educational writing: learning objectives, knowledge structure, instructional clarity, reader transformation.

### Business and entrepreneurship
Business concept; opportunity; problem; target customers; value proposition; offering; business model; revenue possibilities; distribution; operations; competitive context; differentiation; resources; risks; validation opportunities.

### Physical products and inventions
Title; premise; problem; intended users; proposed mechanism; functionality; physical characteristics; materials; technology; manufacturing considerations; safety considerations; feasibility (label: established science / plausible engineering / speculative); differentiation; applications.

### Education and learning
Learning concept; audience; learning problem; objectives; methodology; experience; structure; materials; delivery format; assessment; expected outcomes.

### Architecture and spaces
Project title; spatial concept; site or environment; intended users; functional objectives; spatial arrangement; architectural language; materials; lighting; environmental considerations; experience; practical constraints.

### Games and interactive experiences
Title; genre; core premise; target players; game loop; mechanics; rules; progression; interaction; environment; visual direction; replayability; platforms.

### Events and experiences
Title; concept; purpose; audience; venue or environment; experience journey; activities; spatial design; engagement mechanisms; operations; production considerations.

### Social impact and civic innovation
Project title; social problem; affected population; context; proposed intervention; delivery model; stakeholders; accessibility; implementation; intended outcomes; impact measurement; sustainability.

### Marketing and growth (not templated in the master spec; use this construction)
Campaign title; objective; audience and behaviour insight; core idea; channels; mechanics; sequencing; measurement; risks.

### Unlisted disciplines
Construct a brief by asking: what must someone know to start making this? Typically: the premise, who or what it serves, what makes it distinctive, the experience or output, key components, constraints and feasibility, and first steps. Name your construction in one line so the user can adjust it.
