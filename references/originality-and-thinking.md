# Originality, Thinking Methods and Evaluation

## 1. Originality rules
An idea needs an identifiable reason to exist: a meaningful premise, interesting perspective, distinctive mechanism, unusual application, specific audience, creative interpretation or opportunity.

### Anti-generic checklist (reject before presenting)
- Recycled startup concepts ("Uber for X", "marketplace for Y")
- Generic AI-powered apps whose only differentiator is "AI"
- Predictable mobile-app suggestions
- Superficial variations of existing products
- Familiar film plots with no angle
- Generic brand concepts and repetitive ad ideas
- Vague business opportunities with no customer or mechanism
- Ideas that sound interesting but have no coherent premise
- Buzzword combinations (AI + blockchain + metaverse)
- Ten ideas that are really one idea in ten costumes

### Look beyond the first answer
Overlooked audiences; unusual customer behaviour; underexplored industries; unexpected combinations; alternative distribution; new creative formats; unconventional mechanisms; existing problems approached differently; opportunities hidden in limitations; intersections between disciplines.

### Do not overclaim
Never say "nobody has done this" without evidence. Say "I found no close equivalent in what I checked" (only if you checked), or "I haven't verified novelty".

## 2. Multidisciplinary resources
Draw on game theory, behavioural economics, psychology, sociology, anthropology, systems thinking, design thinking, engineering, architecture, economics, strategy, computer science, cultural studies, storytelling, marketing, scientific research, HCI, emerging technology. Use them as thinking tools; avoid unnecessary academic jargon in the output.

## 3. Beyond existing categories
Great opportunities often sit between industries (agriculture + fintech + logistics; healthcare + behavioural science + gaming; education + interactive storytelling; construction + data intelligence; photography + generative art). Look for non-obvious intersections.

## 4. Novelty is not value
Apply the standard that fits the discipline:
- Art: originality, emotional impact, aesthetic expression. No business model required.
- SaaS: real problem, useful function, plausible business opportunity.
- Social intervention: relevance, accessibility, potential impact.
- Film: premise, character, theme, execution potential.

## 5. Thinking methods (use naturally; do not lecture on them)
1. **Opportunity discovery:** find overlooked opportunities in industries, audiences, technologies, workflows.
2. **Reverse innovation:** start from a limitation and solve it a completely different way.
3. **First principles:** break the problem into fundamentals and question assumptions.
4. **Analogical thinking:** borrow mechanisms from unrelated fields.
5. **Contrarian thinking:** test whether the accepted approach is actually best.
6. **Constraint-based creativity:** near-zero budget? no app? existing infrastructure? radical simplification?
7. **Combination thinking:** join concepts, industries, technologies or disciplines.
8. **Reverse engineering:** study how something works; extract mechanisms and alternatives.
9. **Behavioural observation:** what people do, not only what they say.
10. **Future-oriented thinking:** emerging tech, demographics, behaviours, industry shifts.

## 6. Idea card (for the ten ideas)
Use proportionate detail. Include as relevant to the discipline:
- **Title** (descriptive, memorable)
- **Central concept** (what it is, in 1-2 sentences)
- **What it proposes** (the mechanism or creative move)
- **Problem or opportunity**
- **Intended audience**
- **Distinctive element** (what the obvious version lacks)
- **Why it is interesting**
Aim for roughly 60-120 words per card unless the user wants more. Ensure the ten differ by mechanism, audience, format or angle, not just by name.

## 7. Idea directions (the ten before the ideas)
Each: title, short explanation, what it explores, why it is interesting. Chosen dynamically from the user's objective, industry, audience, research context. Illustrative only (never reuse as a stock list): Underserved Professional Workflows; AI-Native Business Infrastructure; Invisible Operational Problems; Fragmented Industry Services; Behavioural Technology; New Financial Infrastructure; Data Accessibility; Human-AI Collaboration; Emerging Market Solutions; Unconventional Digital Experiences.

## 8. Private evaluation dimensions
Originality; specificity; relevance; differentiation; conceptual coherence; potential value; practicality (where applicable); audience relevance; creative strength; development potential.
- Apply discipline-appropriate criteria.
- Do not show numeric scores unless asked.
- If asked to rank, state the criteria and rank transparently.
- Never claim certainty where there is uncertainty.
