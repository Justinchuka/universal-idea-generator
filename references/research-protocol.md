# Research Protocol (Path 2 and any evidence claim)

## Principle
Investigate what is actually happening. If you did not look, do not imply you did.

## Method
1. Define the industry, discipline or problem area.
2. Identify the relevant market and audience.
3. Investigate existing solutions.
4. Collect evidence of recurring problems or unmet needs.
5. Identify gaps and limitations.
6. Investigate existing attempts to solve them.
7. Look for overlooked opportunities.
8. Identify potential solutions or creative opportunities.
9. Evaluate whether the opportunity is meaningful.
10. Present findings and proposed directions.

Scale effort to the question: a quick check for a small claim, many searches for a market-gap study. Search each distinct item separately rather than in one broad query.

## Choosing sources (examples, not a checklist)
- **Startups and business:** Y Combinator, startup directories, founder discussions, industry reports, company sites, business publications.
- **Customer pain points:** Reddit, relevant communities, product reviews, customer forums, professional discussions.
- **Product research:** Product Hunt, G2, Capterra, App Store, Google Play, GitHub, product websites.
- **Market and trend:** Google Search and Trends, industry reports, government publications, professional associations, news.
- **Creative research:** Behance, Dribbble, Vimeo, YouTube, Pinterest, creative communities, portfolios.
Prefer original and authoritative sources over aggregators. Use recent sources when the field moves fast.

## Evidence standards: label every claim as one of
- **Verified fact** (checked against a reliable source)
- **Direct user feedback** (what real users said, attributed)
- **Repeated observation** (pattern across several sources; say how many)
- **Research-based interpretation** (your reading of the evidence)
- **Hypothesis** (yours, untested)
- **Speculative opportunity**

Never invent statistics, quotes, complaints, market sizes or gaps. Cite sources where available. Quote sparingly and only briefly; paraphrase otherwise.

## No live research available
State at the start: "I can't browse here, so this is not based on live evidence." Then offer labelled hypotheses and a validation plan (what to search, where, what would confirm or kill the idea). Do not present these as findings.

## Output: opportunity context (before ideas)
What was investigated; what was found; what is currently being done; what appears missing; the identified opportunity; why it deserves exploration. Keep it tight and cited.

## Research QA
Research actually conducted? Sources relevant? Findings represented accurately? Facts separated from assumptions? Opportunity logically derived from evidence?
